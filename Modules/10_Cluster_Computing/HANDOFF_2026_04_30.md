### JAX-GPU end-to-end verdict (ladder §14)

Both head-to-head jobs (`9252945 iso_m3_cpu` vs `9252946 iso_m3_gpu`)
timed out at 12h without convergence. Wall-time progress comparison:

| Metric @ 12h | CPU (numpy 32-core) | GPU (JAX vmap) |
|---|---|---|
| Calls | 356,300 | 330,300 |
| log_Z | +31,229.40 | +31,226.56 |
| Time-on-likelihood | 100% | ~93% (CPU 7.9% ahead) |

The 800× per-call vmap speedup measured in the probe (job 9252282)
**does not carry through** to a real Nautilus fit at our 9k-pixel
multi-plane R5 scale. Reasons: Nautilus's `n_batch=50` (vs probe's
batch=256), Python state-management overhead between batches
(identical CPU work for both backends), JAX recompiles at each new
bound-network transition (~5s pause every 1–2 minutes). At our scale,
overhead dominates compute saving.

**Bottom line:** for the curriculum's heavy fits, JAX-GPU offers no
end-to-end advantage over numpy 32-core. Continue using numpy as the
default. Switch to JAX-GPU only when problem size grows enough to
dominate the overhead — likely real HST 100k-pixel cutouts in
`agel_real_target/`, or pixelized-source variants. Probe code for
re-runs lives at `Modules/10_Cluster_Computing/scripts/probe_multiplane_gpu.py`.
